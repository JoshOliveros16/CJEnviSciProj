About land subsidence

What it is, why it happens so slowly, and why Hagonoy is one of the places feeling it most.

Land subsidence is the gradual sinking of the ground surface. It is called a slow-onset disaster because it creeps along year after year and is often ignored until roads, homes and rice fields are already underwater.

Slow and steady

A few centimeters a year looks small, but after ten years it adds up to about a meter. That is enough to turn a dry street into a tidal channel.

Relative sea-level rise

When land drops, the sea seems to rise even if the water itself has not changed. Researchers reported that Manila Bay rose about one meter relative to the land between 1964 and 2002, an effect they linked to subsidence. Philippine Star, 2006

Why Hagonoy is on the front line

  • Low and coastal. Hagonoy sits about 50 km north of Manila, beside northern Manila Bay. Mongabay, 2025
  • Built by sediment. It lies on a delta plain where water and sediments collect, so it floods easily even before any pumping. Mongabay, 2025
  • Heavy pumping. Yearly groundwater extraction in Hagonoy is estimated at about 9 million m³, which may account for around 81 mm of sinking per year. Maclang et al., 2026
  • Sinking everywhere. Areas far from pumping stations are also subsiding, so the problem covers the whole town and the region around it. Maclang et al., 2026

Timeline of a sinking delta

  1. 1991 to 2002

    Parts of Pampanga, Bulacan and Camanava sink by as much as a meter in about 11 years. Philippine Star, 2006

  2. 2003 onward

    Satellite monitoring shows Bulacan and Pampanga sinking 4 to 6 cm every year. AFP/Inquirer, 2019

  3. 2004

    Groundwater extraction is banned in parts of Bulacan. AFP/Khaleej Times, 2025

  4. 2014 to 2020

    Radar satellite analysis finds a maximum of 10.9 cm per year in Bulacan. Sulapas et al., 2024

  5. July 2023

    Hagonoy is placed under a state of calamity after days of flooding. Manila Bulletin, 2023

  6. September 2026

    Floodwater still covers parts of the town, such as Barangay Iba, after weeks of monsoon rain. Daily Tribune, 2026

How fast is it sinking?

Reported rates from different places, methods and periods. They are not directly comparable, but they all point the same way: down.

Hagonoy-Calumpit, average within 500 m of pumping stations Maclang et al.7.0 cm/yr
Hagonoy municipal engineer's estimate GMA~10 cm/yr
Fastest-sinking zone in Bulacan, Guiguinto to Bulakan and Balagtas Sulapas et al.10.9 cm/yr
Hagonoy-Calumpit peak near high-extraction zones Maclang et al.12.45 cm/yr

Did you know? Subsidence does not make it rain. It makes every flood deeper and longer, because the land has less height to spare. Heavy rain, high tides and silted waterways still matter too.
