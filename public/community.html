Community impact

Behind every number is a family, a classroom or a small business. Persistent floodwater changes daily life in many ways at once.

Livelihoods

Farming, fishponds, small stores and daily work stop when streets and fields stay underwater.

Education

Classes are disrupted and children wade or ride through floods to reach school.

Homes and belongings

Floodwater ruins appliances, furniture and documents, and families repeat the cleanup.

Health

Standing water raises the risk of sickness, especially for children and older adults.

Mobility

Boats and raised tricycles replace ordinary transport when roads go under.

Safety

Deep, fast-rising water is dangerous, especially during typhoons and high tides.

Summarized from Mongabay, 2025 and Manila Bulletin, 2023 to 2025

Adapting, but not "used to it"

Residents have invented their own coping tools: boats for everyday travel, elevated tricycles that can cross floodwater, and raised house floors. Some households have moved to safer ground. Mongabay, 2025

A Bulacan youth leader stressed that flooding in Hagonoy is not normal, and that calling people resilient should not become a reason to delay real help. Manila Bulletin, 2025
