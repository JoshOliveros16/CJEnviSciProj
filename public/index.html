Hagonoy GroundWatchThe ground beneath us is sinking. Let’s dig into why.

Hagonoy is a low-lying coastal town beside northern Manila Bay. Studies show that land in and around it is slowly subsiding, sinking by several centimeters every year, mostly because of heavy groundwater pumping. As the land drops, tides and rain reach farther and stay longer.

Scroll down to journey through the layers of the earth, from the surface to bedrock, and learn what is happening, why it matters to our community, and what we can do.

Layer 1 · Topsoil

About Land Subsidence

Land subsidence is the gradual sinking of the ground surface. It is a “slow-onset disaster”: it creeps along year after year, so it is easy to ignore until roads, homes and rice fields are already underwater.

🐌

Slow, steady, hard to see

A few centimeters a year does not look dramatic, but over ten years it adds up to a meter, which is enough to turn a dry street into a tidal channel.

🌊

Relative sea-level rise

When land drops, the sea appears to rise even if the water itself has not. Researchers report that Manila Bay rose about one meter relative to the land between 1964 and 2002, an effect they linked to subsidence. Philippine Star, 2006

Why Hagonoy is on the front line

  • Low and coastal. Hagonoy sits about 50 km north of Manila, beside northern Manila Bay and the Hagonoy River system. Mongabay, 2025
  • Built by sediment. It lies on a delta plain where soft water-laid sediments pile up. These compact naturally and are naturally flood-prone, even before any pumping. Mongabay, 2025
  • Heavy pumping. Hagonoy’s yearly groundwater extraction is estimated at about 9 million m³, possibly enough to account for ~81 mm/year of sinking. Maclang et al., 2026
  • Sinking everywhere. Even areas far from pumping stations are subsiding, so it is a town-wide (and region-wide) problem, not just a local one. Maclang et al., 2026

Timeline of a sinking delta

  1. 1991–2002

    Parts of Pampanga, Bulacan and Camanava sink by as much as a meter in about 11 years. Philstar, 2006

  2. 2003 onward

    Satellite monitoring shows Bulacan and Pampanga sinking 4–6 cm every year. AFP/Inquirer, 2019

  3. 2004

    Groundwater extraction is banned in parts of Bulacan. AFP/Khaleej Times, 2025

  4. 2014–2020

    InSAR analysis finds a maximum of 10.9 cm/year in Bulacan. Sulapas et al., 2024

  5. July 2023

    Hagonoy is placed under a state of calamity after days of floods. Manila Bulletin, 2023

  6. Sept 2026

    Floodwaters still submerge parts of the town, such as Barangay Iba, after weeks of monsoon rain. Daily Tribune, 2026

Pebbo says: Subsidence does not make it rain. It makes every flood deeper and longer, because the land has less height to spare. Heavy rain, high tides and silted waterways still matter too.

Layer 2 · Subsoil

Causes

Several forces push the land down together. In Hagonoy and neighboring towns, groundwater extraction stands out as the biggest human-made driver.

💧

Groundwater extraction

Underground water in the aquifer holds up the soil grains around it. When wells pump water out faster than nature refills it, the pressure drops and the layers above, especially soft clay, squeeze together like a sponge. The ground surface follows.

~70 mm/yraverage sinking within 500 m of pumping stations
~65 mm/yraverage farther away, so it is still sinking, just a little slower

Hagonoy–Calumpit, 2015 – 2021 Q1 (−70.22 vs −65.31 mm/yr). Built-up volume and groundwater withdrawal together explained most of the sinking pattern (R² ≈ 0.85). Maclang et al., 2026

How pumping makes the land sink

  1. Wells pump

    Homes, farms, fishponds and industries draw water from deep wells.

  2. Pressure drops

    With less water between the grains, the aquifer can’t hold up the layers above.

  3. Clay compacts

    Soft clay and silt squeeze together. This is largely permanent.

  4. Land sinks

    The surface lowers, so the same tide or rain now reaches higher and lasts longer.

Other things that add to it

🏗️

Urban growth & weight

More buildings, roads and fishpond dikes mean more load on soft ground and more water demand.

🪨

Natural compaction

Delta sediments slowly settle under their own weight. It is a natural background process that pumping speeds up.

🏞️

Sediment starvation

Dikes and dredging keep river silt from spreading over the plain, so the land no longer rebuilds its lost height. Philstar, 2006

🌡️

Climate change

Rising seas and stronger storms meet land that is already sinking. Sulapas et al., 2024

Layer 3 · Clay

Effects

When the ground drops, the damage shows up where people live: flooded streets, cracked structures and salty water where there used to be dry land.

🌊

Flooding

Hagonoy floods from high tides, monsoon rain and river overflow, and subsidence makes each one worse by lowering the land they spill onto.

  • In July 2023, 12 villages waded in nearly 5 ft (4.86 ft) of tide-driven floodwater for about a week. Manila Bulletin
  • On July 30, 2023, the whole municipality was placed under a state of calamity (Resolution 2023-87). Manila Bulletin
  • Residents have faced high-tide floods for more than a decade. Manila Bulletin
🏠

Infrastructure damage

Roads, bridges, schools, chapels and homes sit lower every year. Fixes tend to be temporary because the ground keeps moving.

  • Road-raising projects of about 1 meter may only buy years: at ~10 cm/yr, roughly a decade. Medium feature
  • Some families raise their homes or give up flooded ground floors; others leave. UP Resilience Institute
  • The municipal engineer said piecemeal flood projects had little effect and called for a master plan with neighboring towns. GMA
🧂

Salty water & land loss

As the bay creeps inland, brackish water can spoil soil and groundwater, hurting farms, fishponds and drinking supplies.

  • Scientists warn that one meter of subsidence can let tides reach 10–20 km farther inland on very gentle coastal plains. Philstar, 2006
  • At least 5,000 people have been displaced from coastal areas north of Manila as the bay advanced. AFP

Layer 4 · Water table

Community Impact

Behind every number is a family, a classroom or a small business. Persistent floodwater in Hagonoy disrupts everyday life in many ways at once.

🐟

Livelihoods

Farming, fishponds, small stores and daily work get interrupted when streets and fields stay underwater.

🎒

Education

Classes are disrupted and children wade (or ride) through floods to reach school.

🛋️

Homes & belongings

Floodwater destroys appliances, furniture and documents, and families repeat the cleanup.

🩺

Health

Standing water raises the risk of sickness, especially for children and older adults.

🚣

Mobility

Boats, raised tricycles and elevated walkways replace ordinary transport when roads go under.

🛟

Safety

Deep, fast-rising water threatens safety, especially during typhoons and high tides.

Impacts summarized from Mongabay, 2025 and Manila Bulletin, 2023–2025

Adapting, but not “used to it”

Hagonoy residents have invented their own coping tools: boats as everyday transport, elevated tricycles that can cross floodwater, and raised house floors. Some households have moved to safer ground. Mongabay, 2025

Local youth leaders stress that flooding in Hagonoy is not normal and that resilience should not be an excuse to delay concrete help. Manila Bulletin, 2025

Layer 5 · Aquifer

Mitigation & Solutions

Geologist Mahar Lagmay has said subsidence could be reversible with effective policies governing the over-digging of wells. Nothing works alone. Community, government and sustainable practices have to work together.

🤝 Community actions

  • Save water at home. Fix leaks, reuse water, and collect rain for non-drinking uses to reduce demand on wells.
  • Report unregistered deep wells to the barangay or LGU, since groundwater use generally needs a permit.
  • Keep waterways clear. Join clean-ups of canals, creeks and drains; garbage worsens flooding.
  • Plan for floods. Know evacuation routes, raise appliances and documents, and follow local advisories.
  • Document and report. Photos and dates of flooding, cracks and sinking help local planners; use the report form on this site.

🏛️ Government initiatives

  • Groundwater limits. Extraction has been banned in parts of Bulacan since 2004; the NWRB is tasked to enforce water-permit rules. AFP; Philstar
  • Monitoring. NAMRIA provided 21 benchmarks in Hagonoy and Calumpit, and GNSS stations (including one in Sto. Niño, Hagonoy) track vertical movement. ISPRS, 2026
  • North Manila Bay Flood Protection Strategy. A Netherlands-funded effort that, with the Provincial Government of Bulacan, is exploring nature-based solutions. PGB
  • Master planning. Hagonoy’s engineer urges a flood-mitigation and water-management plan with Paombong, Malolos and Calumpit. GMA
  • Land-use planning. Researchers recommend building subsidence risk into zoning and local plans. Maclang et al.

🌱 Sustainable practices

  • Alternative water sources. Shift supply from deep wells to treated surface water or piped systems to ease aquifer pumping.
  • Nature-based defenses. Restore mangroves and coastal vegetation to slow waves and trap sediment. Inquirer; PGB
  • Watershed care. Reforestation and watershed conservation reduce runoff and silt problems. Inquirer, 2023
  • Let water in. Use permeable surfaces, retention areas and clean drains rather than sealing every surface with concrete.
  • Measure and share. Community and school-based monitoring keep the issue visible.

Pebbo says: Solutions that only raise the ground (roads, floors) treat the symptom. The long-term fix is to slow the sinking itself by managing how much groundwater we take.

Is land subsidence the same as sea-level rise?

No. Sea-level rise is the water getting higher; subsidence is the land getting lower. In Hagonoy both happen, and the combined effect is called relative sea-level rise. For a low coastal town, a sinking land surface can matter as much as the sea itself.

Can subsidence be stopped or reversed?

It can be slowed by limiting groundwater pumping and managing land use. Geologist Mahar Lagmay has said it could be reversible with effective well-digging policies. In general, though, compacted clay does not fully spring back, so prevention matters most.

Is rain the only reason Hagonoy floods?

No. Rain, typhoons and monsoon winds trigger floods, but high tides, river overflow, silted channels, drainage problems and sinking land all add up. Subsidence does not create rainfall; it makes floodwater deeper and slower to drain.

Is it legal to drill my own deep well?

Under the Water Code of the Philippines (Presidential Decree No. 1067), groundwater use generally requires a permit from the National Water Resources Board (NWRB). If you suspect an unregistered deep well, tell your barangay or LGU.

How do I know if my barangay is affected?

Ask your barangay officials or the Municipal Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (MDRRMO), and check official hazard maps from government agencies. The map tab here shows only areas named in news reports.

What can a student or resident do right now?

Save water, keep drains clear, learn evacuation routes, report observations through the form here, and share what you learn with family and neighbors. Awareness is the first step toward pressure for better policy.

Why does a community report form matter?

Satellite data shows how fast land moves, but only residents know where water enters homes first, where roads crack and which wells are running. Local observations can guide where officials look next.
